## Evaluation

**Factual errors**

1. **"RAM is permanent memory that stores data even when the computer is turned off."** Incorrect. RAM (Random Access Memory) is *volatile*: it loses its contents when power is removed.

2. **"ROM is temporary memory that loses its data when power is removed."** Incorrect. ROM (Read-Only Memory) is *non-volatile*: it retains its contents without power.

The student has swapped the two definitions, so the central point of the answer is reversed.

**What is correct**

- "Both are used to store information in a computer." This is true, though it is very general and adds little to the comparison.

**What a complete answer would include**

| Feature | RAM | ROM |
|---|---|---|
| Volatility | Volatile (data lost without power) | Non-volatile (data retained) |
| Read/write | Read and write, fast | Mostly read-only (or rarely rewritten) |
| Purpose | Holds running programs and data currently in use | Stores permanent instructions such as firmware or the BIOS/boot code |
| Speed and capacity | Faster, larger (GBs) | Slower, smaller |

**Score: 1/5**

**Justification:** The question asks specifically for the *difference*, and the student gets the key distinction (volatile vs. non-volatile) exactly backwards. The only accurate statement is the generic closing sentence, which does not distinguish the two. The answer also omits purpose, read/write capability, and examples. The single mark reflects the correct general idea that both are memory types, and the fact that the student at least attempted to frame the answer around permanence versus temporariness, which is the right axis of comparison even though it was misapplied.
