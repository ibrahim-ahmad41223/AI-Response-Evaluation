**Machine learning** is a way for computers to learn patterns from examples (data) so they can make useful decisions or predictions on new situations—without someone writing exact step-by-step rules for every possible case.

### Simple idea
Instead of programming a computer with rigid instructions like “if the email contains the word ‘lottery’ then call it spam,” you show it many examples of spam and non-spam emails. The computer figures out the patterns itself. The more good examples it sees, the better it usually gets.

### One everyday example: your email spam filter
1. You (or other people) mark some emails as “spam” and others as “not spam.”
2. The computer looks at thousands of these labeled emails and notices patterns—certain words, senders, links, writing styles, etc.
3. When a new email arrives, it uses what it learned to guess: “This looks like spam” or “This looks normal.”
4. If it’s wrong and you correct it, that becomes another example that helps it improve over time.

That’s supervised learning in action: the computer is shown the “right answers” during training and then applies the patterns to new data.

In short: machine learning turns lots of examples into a system that can recognize patterns and make predictions, the way a spam filter learns to protect your inbox.
