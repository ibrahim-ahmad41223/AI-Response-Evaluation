**How World War I Began**

Long ago, in 1914, Europe was split into two groups of powerful countries that had promised to defend each other. One group was France, Russia, and Britain. The other was Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy. The countries were also building bigger armies and competing for land and influence, which made everyone nervous (Encyclopaedia Britannica).

The spark came on June 28, 1914, in Sarajevo. Archduke Franz Ferdinand, the heir to Austria-Hungary's throne, was shot and killed by Gavrilo Princip, a young man who wanted Bosnian Serbs freed from Austro-Hungarian rule. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and declared war on it on July 28 (Imperial War Museums).

Then the promises set off a chain reaction, like falling dominoes. Russia backed Serbia, and Germany backed Austria-Hungary. Germany declared war on Russia and France, then invaded neutral Belgium. Britain joined the war on August 4, 1914. A small conflict had become a world war (Encyclopaedia Britannica; National WWI Museum and Memorial).
