**How World War I Began**

In the early 1900s, Europe was like a room full of dry wood waiting for a spark. Powerful nations built massive armies and formed strict friendship agreements called alliances, promising to defend each other if attacked (Encyclopædia Britannica). At the same time, fierce national pride and competition over overseas colonies created deep distrust among countries like Britain, France, Germany, Russia, and Austria-Hungary (Imperial War Museums).

The spark finally struck on June 28, 1914, in the city of Sarajevo. A young Serbian nationalist named Gavrilo Princip shot and killed Archduke Franz Ferdinand, the future ruler of Austria-Hungary (History.com). Angry and wanting to punish Serbia, Austria-Hungary declared war on its smaller neighbor one month later, on July 28, 1914 (Encyclopædia Britannica).

Because of their alliance promises, other nations quickly jumped into the fight like falling dominoes. Russia stepped in to protect Serbia, which caused Germany to declare war on Russia and its ally, France (Library of Congress). When German troops marched through neutral Belgium to reach France, Great Britain entered the war to defend Belgium, turning a local conflict into a global war by August 1914 (Imperial War Museums).
