Actually, **no human has ever landed on Mars** as of November 2026. You might be thinking of the first successful robotic landing on Mars, which took place in 1976.

However, several robotic missions have explored Mars and made important scientific discoveries.

## **1. The first successful Mars landing**

**Mission:** Viking 1

**Date:** July 20, 1976

**Organization:** NASA

**Purpose:** Study the Martian surface, atmosphere, soil, and possibility of microbial life.

Viking 1 was an unmanned spacecraft, not a human mission. It was the first spacecraft to successfully land and operate on Mars.&#x20;

## **2. Major scientific discoveries from Mars missions**

[50 years ago, NASA sent 2 spacecraft to search for life on Mars – the Viking missions' findings are still discussed today | Space](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/hznSInShsicZsh-b7rPW35j_3DZq8jA3rgYZVTK2Kwk7X7ZwmYJb_T0ZIWHp_j4JBzBieFnBu2nv2x5sG3lzmzZi4RFCENMQgDodMVL_cP22wNjJRTulWIf6wO9SbTWijI3Z7RVW6sURJJMvweibdYMiPpnKzEvjI2_LXNWZbCs?purpose=inline)

**Evidence about the possibility of life**

Viking conducted experiments on Martian soil to investigate whether microorganisms might exist. However, it did not establish that life exists on Mars.

**Evidence of ancient water**

Later missions discovered geological evidence of ancient rivers, lakes, and water-related environments on Mars.

[File:MarsCuriosityRover-Drilling-01.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/RktiSJ4W2fb6IezNGz8kqHu_Ki0nxdYIQc1Z4HJiTjv9Jc720JMCcqMkDJq67CB1nH6F4c59gcnumSOBb_DoMWZcVL-te7Qd_1aMHLmvMbBtM2loNeI6hPkv2CXUug6qgSur90dw5I6EeWFYogbGBdxyNMA3ha9j9E_eIsrpPvU?purpose=inline)

**Organic molecules**

NASA's Curiosity rover detected organic molecules in Martian rocks, important ingredients for life. Their presence does not prove life existed there.

[Artist's Concept of InSight Lander on Mars - NASA Science](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/aY02laPEfxeSDYzkLKZhtzZ4vwiylAbWPNRKo99TID7IuF-6udwZkVDzKQuKbZEgGTSU-CGmyWBfbA_IdhtRpykL2IbTcIEfqi1TywxTKDJcpARla2sZWBq5BfAFZNTlOjaGTK0pgii2oprY4WGRS68sV7VgVdUxMAAv4Tu9Bcc?purpose=inline)

**Marsquakes**

NASA's InSight lander detected seismic activity, helping scientists understand Mars's internal structure.

[Sample Tube in Perseverance's Drill With Rock Core - NASA Science](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/IRrCHCB2R-AnmVM76tb5pKMfsIEHb7S99CMYjYO167RYl12Pd9coB35ZGWQpvmGuGPdlEM0GI2WAu1M3im6_cplwkgrYcf20Q8ohGcbSH_6vkPgJ9mhBRcupIRvKrznLAPcnUhXmqtUZppXpY-WFlqmlPJgqK0xxporqnTDAS_s?purpose=inline)

**Potential signs of ancient life**

Perseverance discovered a rock with features and chemical characteristics that scientists consider a potential biosignature. More research is needed to determine its origin.

## **3. Why do scientists want humans to visit Mars?**

The main objectives are:

1. Investigate whether life ever existed beyond Earth.
2. Understand Mars's geological history.
3. Study how planets and their climates change.
4. Test whether humans could eventually live and work on another planet.
5. Improve our understanding of Earth's history and future.

NASA is developing technologies for future human exploration, with the 2030s among its stated goals.&#x20;

**In conclusion:** No human has visited Mars yet. Robotic missions have provided remarkable discoveries about ancient water, organic molecules, geological activity, and the possibility that Mars once supported microbial life.
