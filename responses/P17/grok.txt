**No human has ever landed on Mars.** As of November 2026, all successful Mars landings have been by robotic spacecraft (landers and rovers). Human missions remain in the planning stage, with the earliest realistic crewed landings projected for the early 2030s at best (e.g., SpaceX estimates of people on Mars in roughly 5–7 years from mid-2026).

### Robotic firsts (for context)
- The Soviet **Mars 3** achieved the first soft landing in December 1971, but it failed after only about 20 seconds and returned almost no useful data.
- NASA’s **Viking 1** (July 20, 1976) was the first fully successful long-duration lander. It (with Viking 2 a few weeks later) returned the first clear surface images, weather data, soil analyses, and biological experiments. Those missions operated for years and established that the surface is dry, rocky, and hostile to Earth-like life as tested, while confirming past water-related geology.

Later robotic missions (Pathfinder/Sojourner, Spirit/Opportunity, Curiosity, Perseverance, China’s Tianwen-1/Zhurong, etc.) have greatly expanded knowledge of Mars’ geology, climate history, water ice, organic molecules, and potential past habitability—but none involved humans.

### Why humans have not landed yet
A crewed Mars landing is far more complex and expensive than robotic ones. It requires:
- Reliable heavy-lift and interplanetary propulsion (Starship-class systems are still in testing).
- Life support, radiation protection, and medical capabilities for a multi-year round trip.
- Entry, descent, and landing of much larger vehicles.
- Surface habitats, power, and resources (ISRU for propellant/oxygen is still experimental).
- Political will, sustained funding, and international coordination.

NASA, SpaceX, and others continue precursor work (rovers collecting samples, technology demonstrators like MOXIE for oxygen production, orbiters studying the environment), but no crewed landing has occurred.

In short: the premise of the question is incorrect—there has been no first human landing on Mars, so there are no associated human scientific discoveries from such a mission. Robotic exploration continues to advance our understanding in the meantime.
