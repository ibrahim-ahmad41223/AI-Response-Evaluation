No human has ever landed on Mars yet. All exploration of the Martian surface to date has been conducted by uncrewed robotic landers and rovers.

### Why Humans Have Not Landed Yet

* **Current Status:** Space agencies like NASA and private companies like SpaceX are actively developing the heavy-lift rockets, life-support systems, and entry-descent-landing technologies needed for a crewed trip, with preliminary targets aimed at the 2030s or 2040s.
* **Major Hurdles:** A round-trip mission takes roughly two to three years and requires overcoming severe deep-space radiation, prolonged microgravity, massive fuel requirements, and the difficulty of safely landing a heavy crewed habitat through Mars's thin atmosphere.

### Key Robotic Landings and Discoveries

While humans haven't set foot on the planet, robotic missions have made major scientific breakthroughs:

* **First Successful Landings (1970s):** The Soviet Union's **Mars 3** (1971) was the first spacecraft to achieve a soft landing on Mars, though it lost contact seconds later. NASA's **Viking 1 and Viking 2** (1976) were the first fully successful surface missions, returning the first color photos of the landscape and conducting soil experiments to search for microbial life.
* **Evidence of Ancient Water (1990s–2010s):** Rovers like **Spirit, Opportunity, and Curiosity** discovered minerals (such as hematite and clays) and sedimentary rock layers proving that Mars once had standing liquid water, lakes, and flowing rivers capable of supporting microbial life.
* **Organic Molecules & Active Geology (2010s–2020s):** **Curiosity** and **Perseverance** detected complex organic carbon molecules—the chemical building blocks of life—preserved in ancient mudstones. Meanwhile, the **InSight** lander detected hundreds of "marsquakes," revealing that Mars is still seismically active and helping scientists map its crust, mantle, and liquid iron core.
* **Resource Utilization & Flight (2020s):** **Perseverance** successfully generated breathable oxygen directly from Mars's carbon-dioxide-rich atmosphere using an onboard instrument called MOXIE, and deployed **Ingenuity**, the first aircraft to achieve powered, controlled flight on another planet.
