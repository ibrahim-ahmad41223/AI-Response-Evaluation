Yes, it's very normal. Milestone birthdays tend to make people take stock, and 30 in particular carries a lot of cultural weight. It's often the point where people start comparing where they are with where they thought they'd be by now, and that comparison rarely feels kind. Feeling flat, wistful, or even a bit sad on a day that's "supposed" to be happy doesn't mean something is wrong with you. Often it just means you're noticing time passing, and that can sting even when life is going fine.

Some things that might help:

**Figure out what the feeling is actually about.** "Not happy about 30" can mean different things: grief over time passing, disappointment about career, relationships, or money, pressure from family or peers, or just a general sense of "is this it?" Naming the specific thing usually makes it smaller and more workable than a vague cloud of dread.

**Be wary of the "timeline" comparison.** The idea that you should have certain things sorted by 30 is mostly a story we absorb, not a rule. Plenty of people hit their stride, change careers, find partners, or start over in their 30s, 40s, and beyond.

**Let the feeling be there without forcing a celebration.** You don't have to be cheerful just because it's your birthday. Do something low-pressure that you genuinely enjoy, like a good meal, a walk, or time with someone who makes you feel at ease.

**Do a gentle inventory.** Write down a few things you've done, learned, or survived in your twenties that you wouldn't have predicted, and a few things you'd like the next decade to include. This turns the day from a verdict into a starting point.

**Talk to someone.** A friend, family member, or anyone you trust can help, since this kind of feeling often shrinks when said out loud.

If the heaviness lingers for weeks, or starts to affect your sleep, appetite, motivation, or interest in things you usually enjoy, it's worth talking to a counselor or doctor. A birthday dip is normal; persistent low mood deserves some support.
