**Pause, verify independently, communicate calmly, and protect yourself—do not donate under group pressure.**

Treat the situation as a potential social-engineering risk first. Group momentum and “everyone’s doing it” are classic pressure tactics that scammers exploit; the fact that a friend forwarded it does not make the request legitimate.

### How I would approach it
1. **Refuse the immediate pressure.** I would not click links, open attachments, or transfer money while the group chat is active and emotional. I would reply with something neutral and non-confrontational such as: “I’ll look into this properly and get back—don’t want to rush a donation.” Silence or a short delay is better than a hasty yes.

2. **Verify offline and independently.**  
   - Ignore the forwarded message’s links, QR codes, or account details.  
   - Search for the claimed organization or cause through official channels (their real website, verified social accounts, charity registries, or news sources).  
   - Cross-check the exact campaign, bank details, or payment request against those official sources.  
   - Look for common cybercrime red flags: urgency language, new or unverified accounts, requests for unusual payment methods (gift cards, crypto, personal transfers), mismatched branding, or pressure to act before verification is possible.

3. **Assess the social dynamic privately.** I would message one or two close friends in the group one-on-one rather than challenging the whole chat. Frame it as caution, not accusation: “I’m seeing some red flags on this—has anyone confirmed the official page/details?” This reduces the risk of group backlash while still raising the issue.

4. **Decide based on evidence, not consensus.** If the request cannot be independently verified as going to a legitimate, transparent cause, I would not donate. Peer pressure does not override personal risk assessment. If it checks out, I would use an official, traceable channel and keep records.

### Practical advice to give (or follow yourself)
- **Never donate via forwarded links or chat-provided payment details.** Always go to the organization’s official site or a reputable platform (and type the URL yourself).
- **Treat urgency as a warning sign.** Legitimate charities rarely require instant group action with no time to verify.
- **Protect financial and personal data.** Do not share ID, bank screenshots, or one-time passwords “to process the donation.”
- **Communicate without escalating.** A calm “I’m double-checking the source first” is usually enough. If the group pushes hard, step back from the conversation temporarily.
- **If it’s a scam, report it.** Alert the platform, bank (if money moved), and relevant authorities. Warn the original friend privately so they can stop amplifying it.
- **Offer constructive alternatives.** If people want to help the stated cause, suggest verified local or established organizations instead of an unverified appeal.
- **Accept that some friends may still donate.** Your responsibility is your own risk tolerance and due diligence, not controlling the group.

Social proof and friendship can override caution; the disciplined response is to slow the process down, verify outside the chat, and only act on confirmed information. If the source remains untrustworthy, declining is the rational and responsible choice.
