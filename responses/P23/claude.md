Group pressure works in scammers' favor. Urgency and "everyone else is doing it" are classic social engineering tactics, and a forwarded message gives the request borrowed trust: your friend is real, but that doesn't mean the request is. Your friend may also have just forwarded it without checking, so the donations of the whole group could rest on one unverified message.

**How I'd approach it**

1. **Pause.** Nobody has to donate in the next ten minutes. Genuine causes can wait a day for you to check them, and pressure to act immediately is itself a warning sign.
2. **Separate the cause from the channel.** You can care about the cause and still distrust this particular link, account, or payment method. Doubting the channel isn't doubting your friends or being stingy.
3. **Verify independently.** Don't use the links or contact details in the message. Search for the organization yourself, go to its official website by typing the address, and check whether it's a registered charity (many countries have public registries). Look for the same campaign on the organization's own pages.
4. **Check the red flags.** Be wary of requests for payment by gift card, crypto, or wire transfer, personal bank accounts instead of an organization's, vague details about who benefits, emotional or urgent language, lookalike names that mimic well-known charities, and links that don't match the organization's real domain.
5. **Ask your friend where it came from.** A private message works better than a public challenge: "Hey, do you know where this originated? I want to make sure it's legitimate before I donate." Often they'll realize they never checked either.

**What I'd say to the group**

Frame it as caution, not accusation. For example: "I'd love to help, but I always verify donation requests first because there are so many scams. Can someone share the organization's official website? I'll donate directly through that." This is hard to argue with, and it may protect others who hadn't thought to check.

**If the cause is real, give safely**

- Donate through the organization's official website or app, not through the forwarded link.
- Use a credit card or a trusted payment service, which offers dispute options, rather than a direct transfer.
- Keep a receipt, and don't give out more personal information than needed.

**If it looks like a scam**

Tell the group clearly and calmly, without blaming whoever shared it. Anyone who already donated should contact their bank or payment provider right away, since quick action sometimes allows a payment to be reversed, and the scam can be reported to the relevant platform or authorities.

**The underlying principle**

A good friend group should be able to handle "let's check first." If someone reacts badly to verification, that's more worth noticing than the message itself. Real charities welcome scrutiny, and your friends will probably be glad someone asked.
